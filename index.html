TrueClaim — Production Explainable AI

From decision-tree research to a commercial insurance platform.

I led the technical design and productization of an insurance fraud decision platform: interpretable models, reproducible training, real-time scoring and explanations that help claims reviewers understand a prediction.

Python · FastAPI · PostgreSQL · AWS · Databricks · MLflow · Docker

Explore my contribution +

The problem

Insurance fraud detection needs useful predictions alongside consistent explanations, auditable model changes and integration into human review workflows.

My contribution: algorithms and architecture

I independently reimplemented and investigated Tree Alternating Optimization methods from the research of Miguel Á. Carreira-Perpiñán and collaborators, then worked on integrating optimized trees into practical ML systems. I also designed the platform architecture, developed training pipelines and counterfactual explanations, and coordinated engineers and vendors.

Separating application, training and inference

FastAPI, PostgreSQL and Redis/RQ support application workflows and job coordination. Training workloads handle preprocessing, cost-sensitive learning and evaluation, with Databricks and MLflow supporting reproducibility, model registration and lineage. Inference services expose scoring and model-native explanations to the application.

Cloud architecture and lifecycle design

My architecture work covers containerized workloads, AWS storage and compute, tenant isolation, cross-account access and Terraform infrastructure design. Deployment planning considers customer-hosted and platform-hosted workloads, with separate training and serving resources. Lifecycle design includes monitoring, reviewer feedback and retraining.

Explainability with grounded LLM interfaces

The predictive model produces the score; the tree path explains its decision. Counterfactual algorithms calculate feasible changes for an alternative outcome. I built LLM-based narratives grounded in those structured results to support human review.

Outcome

A commercial AI product and the engineering foundations around it, connecting research implementation with customer workflows and technical team leadership.

Explainable AI · ICAIF 2026 · Accepted main track

Actionable Feature Importance

Exact counterfactual algorithms for decisions people can act on.

Which feature can be changed to obtain a desired prediction, and at what cost? With Miguel Á. Carreira-Perpiñán, I researched feature importance based on exact single-feature counterfactual optimization.

Axis-aligned & oblique trees · Constrained optimization · Counterfactual explanations

Explore my contribution +

The problem

An influential feature is not necessarily one a person can feasibly change. An actionable explanation needs to connect a feature’s importance with an achievable alternative prediction.

Research contribution

My work with Miguel Á. Carreira-Perpiñán spans the counterfactual optimization formulation, algorithm implementation and experimental evaluation. Our exact method supports axis-aligned and oblique decision trees, continuous and categorical variables, feasibility constraints and user-defined feature-change costs.

Technical approach and outcome

We calculate the minimum cost of changing a single feature to reach a target prediction, including identifying when that change is impossible. Evaluation compares the resulting importance with LIME, SHAP and permutation importance. The work was accepted to the ICAIF 2026 main track; proceedings are forthcoming.

05 /

UPM & IMDEA Materials · PhD research · Airbus collaboration

Digital Twin — Deep Learning For Manufacturing

Physical simulation meets real-time inference.

I developed deep-learning models that connect expensive physical simulations with limited sensor measurements, enabling a digital twin to detect disturbances and predict resin flow during composite manufacturing.

PyTorch · CNNs · ResNet-style models · Encoder-decoders · Scientific ML

Explore my contribution +

The problem

High-fidelity manufacturing simulations are too expensive for real-time monitoring. Sparse pressure measurements must reveal changes in a complex physical process.

My contribution

I co-designed the digital twin, implemented its learning models and compared predictions against physical experiments. My doctoral research investigated CNN-based disturbance detection, ResNet-style inverse models and encoder-decoder surrogates trained on synthetic data from physical simulators.

Technical approach

The integrated twin estimates manufacturing disturbances from sensor measurements, then predicts pressure and resin flow-front fields using deep surrogate models. This connects inverse estimation and forward prediction in one monitoring system.

Published outcome

The 2024 digital-twin paper reports pressure-field prediction errors below 1% and model consultation times below 50 ms, evaluated against resin-transfer-moulding experiments. I implemented the model and carried out the comparison with experiments conducted by a collaborator.

06 /

Reinforcement learning · Sim-to-real

Reinforcement Learning For Process Control

A controller trained in simulation and evaluated in the laboratory.

I extended digital-twin research into closed-loop control: training a Deep Q-Network in a physical simulation and evaluating the learned policy in laboratory resin-transfer-moulding experiments.

DQN · State/action/reward design · Darcy flow · Sensor feedback · Process control

Explore my contribution +

The problem

A controller must respond to changing material properties and process conditions using limited pressure-sensor observations.

Research and implementation

My work connected reinforcement-learning formulation, simulation-based training and policy evaluation. A Level Set–Darcy environment models variability in fabric permeability and resin viscosity. The DQN uses sensor feedback to adjust inlet pressure and maintain target resin-flow behaviour.

Outcome

The trained policy was evaluated in physical manufacturing experiments, connecting simulation-based learning with laboratory control. This research appears in Composites Part A, volume 202 (2026).
